Technology Publishing Platform

A redesign of an existing technology publishing platform, focused on clearer content discovery, article reading, and thoughtfully integrated advertising placements.

Role

UI/UX Designer - Team Project

Process

Review, design, handoff, implementation support, A/B testing

Tools

Figma

Problem

The platform needed to support a large volume of editorial content while keeping high-value ad placements visible.

Goal

Create a clearer content and reading experience while strategically integrating advertising formats across key pages.

Outcome

A redesigned publishing experience with clearer editorial layouts, planned advertising placements, and responsive pages that adapted across screen sizes.

The challenge

The website published frequent technology stories across several categories. Readers needed to scan stories quickly, move into articles easily, and read without unnecessary friction.

Because the platform attracted a technology-focused audience, advertisers were interested in premium placements that could give their campaigns strong visibility. The publisher also needed clear, sellable inventory that the sales team could offer confidently.

The challenge was to design a structure where editorial content and advertising could work together, instead of competing for attention.

My role and responsibilities

This was a collaborative client project involving design, development, sales, and leadership stakeholders. My background in Ad Operations gave me useful context around how publishers use advertising inventory and why visibility and placement are commercially important.

As part of the design team, I contributed to restructuring the existing publishing experience, particularly around content organization, article layouts, responsive behavior, and the integration of advertising placements.

  • Review: Reviewed existing publishing layouts, content structures, and ad placement patterns.
  • Design: Contributed to layouts that supported homepage, category, article, and advertising requirements.
  • Collaboration: Worked with the wider team through design handoff, implementation support, and A/B testing considerations.

Understanding the existing experience

The website already had an established content structure, category navigation, and advertising model. The redesign was not about starting from scratch; it was about improving how the existing experience organized stories, supported article reading, and created planned spaces for advertising.

Content volume

Frequent publishing meant story lists and category sections needed to stay easy to scan.

Article focus

Article pages needed to keep the selected story as the main focus while supporting related content and advertising.

Ad inventory

Premium and standard ad formats needed predictable spaces that could support advertiser visibility without interrupting the reading path.

Balancing user and business needs

How might we create advertising placements that were valuable for advertisers and the publisher, while maintaining a clear and readable content experience for users?

Design direction

01. Keep content hierarchy clear

Story lists, categories, and article pages needed a clear structure so readers could move through the site without confusion.

02. Plan advertising as part of the layout

Ad placements were designed into the page structure so premium and standard inventory had defined spaces instead of feeling added later.

03. Let high-impact formats adapt

Desktop high-impact ads could create strong initial visibility, then collapse as users moved deeper into the article experience.

Structuring the article experience

When users selected a story from the homepage or a category page, the article page needed to shift from discovery to focused reading.

The structure had to support the article headline, metadata, hero or media area, reading column, standard ad placements, and a related stories grid at the bottom, while keeping the selected story as the main focus.

Creating a high-impact advertising experience

Premium advertising required a highly visible placement that could support large brand campaigns. On desktop, the team used a high-impact ad format at the top of the experience.

The initial 1600 x 800 format gave advertisers strong visibility when users entered the page. As users scrolled, the placement collapsed to 1600 x 400, keeping the campaign visible while returning more vertical space to the content.

This behavior was designed for desktop. On mobile, the layout used responsive page structures with smaller ad formats such as 320 x 50 leaderboards.

The solution

The redesign brought editorial content and advertising into a clearer structure, supporting both reader needs and the publisher's commercial requirements.

01. Structured content hierarchy

Created clearer layouts for organizing stories, categories, and article pages so readers could move from discovery into focused reading.

02. High-impact desktop advertising

Used a prominent desktop ad format that gave advertisers strong initial visibility, then collapsed as users moved through the page.

03. Advertising-aware layouts

Planned standard ad placements such as 970 x 90, 728 x 90, and 320 x 50 into responsive page structures.

04. User + business balance

Considered reader clarity, advertiser visibility, and publisher revenue together instead of treating them as separate goals.

The final experience

The final direction created a publishing experience where content structure and advertising needs were planned together.

Homepage structure

Category sections supported browsing while planned ad slots appeared between content groups.

Article reading experience

Article pages focused on the selected story, with standard ad placements and related stories organized around the reading path.

Responsive ad approach

Desktop supported high-impact ad behavior, while mobile used smaller formats such as 320 x 50 leaderboards within responsive layouts.

Impact and reflection

This project helped me understand how UX decisions in publishing need to account for both the reader experience and the commercial model behind the product.

My previous experience in Ad Operations gave me context around why ad placement, size, and visibility matter to publishers, advertisers, and sales teams. Working within the UX process helped me approach those requirements from a design perspective.

One of my key learnings was that good UX does not always mean removing commercial elements from an interface. In a publishing product, advertising is part of the ecosystem. The design challenge is to integrate it intentionally so business goals, advertiser visibility, and user needs can work together.
