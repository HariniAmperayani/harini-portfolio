Recipe & Shop Platform

At MediaMint, I contributed to redesigning a client's existing recipe and shopping platform, with a focus on recipe content, product discovery, and contextual advertising.

Role

UI/UX Designer - Team Project

Process

Review, structure, design, iterate

Tools

Figma

Problem

The existing Recipe and Shop experiences needed a clearer, more modern structure for content, product browsing, and commercial modules.

Goal

Modernise the recipe and shopping journeys while integrating commercial opportunities in ways that matched each page's purpose.

Outcome

A redesigned experience with structured product grids, clearer recipe layouts, contextual video, and recipe-to-product connections.

The challenge

The platform supported two different user journeys. One focused on discovering and using recipe content, while the other focused on browsing and purchasing kitchen and lifestyle products.

Each journey had different commercial requirements. Recipe content was free to access, so advertising and sponsored content formed part of the monetisation model. The Shop already had a direct commercial purpose through product sales, so advertising was intentionally not included there.

The challenge was to modernise both experiences while giving each one a structure that matched its purpose.

My role & responsibilities

This was a collaborative client project at MediaMint. I worked with one other designer and collaborated with developers, sales, and managers to understand requirements and contribute design solutions for the recipe and Shop experiences.

The work focused on modernising existing layouts rather than creating a new product from scratch.

  • Review: Reviewed existing page structures and content requirements.
  • Recipe layouts: Contributed to redesigned recipe and article page structures.
  • Shop layouts: Improved product-browsing structure through a grid-based Shop layout.
  • Commercial modules: Designed around defined desktop and mobile advertising formats and a contextual video module.
  • Responsive design: Considered how recipe content, Shop grids, and commercial modules would adapt across screen sizes.
  • Collaboration: Worked with the wider team through redesign discussions, feedback, and implementation considerations.

Understanding the existing experience

The platform already had established recipe content, a Shop, advertising inventory, and brand partnerships. The redesign needed to organise these existing elements into a clearer structure for modern browsing behaviour.

Shop experience

Users needed to browse a large catalogue of kitchen-related products such as cookware, kitchen essentials, and kitchen decor.

Recipe content

Recipe pages needed to accommodate the recipe title, imagery, ingredients, editorial content, advertising, video, and relevant products.

Connecting content and commerce

How might we modernise the recipe and shopping experiences while creating natural connections between recipe content, relevant commercial content, and product discovery?

Design direction

01. Make products easier to browse

Use a consistent grid system to organise products and make browsing easier to scan.

02. Give recipe content a clear hierarchy

Structure recipe name, imagery, ingredients, and article content so users can understand the page quickly.

03. Make commercial content relevant

Sponsored video content should relate to the recipe being viewed rather than feeling disconnected.

04. Connect recipes with useful products

When a recipe includes relevant cookware or kitchen products, create a natural path to the Shop.

Making product discovery easier

The existing Shop experience needed a more structured way to present a growing range of cookware, kitchen essentials, and kitchen decor.

The redesigned experience introduced a grid-based product layout, creating a consistent visual structure for browsing across categories on desktop while allowing the grid to adapt for smaller screens. The Shop intentionally remained ad-free because its commercial purpose was already product discovery and purchase.

Structuring the recipe experience

Recipe pages served a different purpose from the Shop. Users arrived to discover a recipe and needed quick access to the most important information while still being able to move through supporting editorial content.

The redesigned layout created a clearer hierarchy around the recipe title, large recipe image, ingredients, content, supporting video, advertising, and relevant products.

Planning advertising around recipe content

Recipe content was free for users to access, so advertising formed part of the commercial model for these pages. Rather than placing ads across every part of the platform, inventory was intentionally incorporated into the recipe experience while the Shop remained ad-free.

To keep the page clear and readable, the design used standard ad sizes instead of heavier pop-up or overlay formats.

Making sponsored content more relevant

The redesign introduced a dedicated video player within the recipe experience. Rather than treating sponsored video as unrelated promotional content, the video could be contextually aligned with the recipe the user was already viewing.

For example, a user reading a mocktail recipe could be shown sponsored content featuring a relevant beverage product, while a user viewing a cake recipe could be shown sponsored content featuring a relevant baking ingredient.

Creating a path from recipe to product

Recipes also created opportunities for relevant product discovery. When a recipe used a specific piece of cookware or kitchen product, users could access related product information and continue to the Shop if they were interested in purchasing it.

This created a natural connection between inspiration and commerce without turning the recipe page into a shopping page.

The solution

01. Structured product discovery

Introduced a grid-based Shop layout to organise kitchen products and support easier product scanning.

02. Clearer recipe hierarchy

Restructured recipe pages around the information users needed most: recipe imagery, ingredients, and content.

03. Contextually relevant sponsored media

Introduced a video module capable of presenting sponsored recipe content related to the user's current recipe.

04. Content-to-commerce journey

Connected recipes with relevant kitchen products, giving users a natural route from inspiration to product discovery and the Shop.

Impact & reflection

This project helped me understand how different parts of the same platform can support different user and business goals.

The Shop focused on product discovery and purchase, while free recipe pages needed to balance useful editorial content with advertising and sponsored opportunities.

One key learning was that commercial content feels more natural when it is relevant to what the user is already doing. The contextual video module and recipe-to-product connections showed how content and commerce could work together without turning every interaction into a direct sales moment.

Working on an existing platform also strengthened my understanding of designing within established content, advertising, and business constraints rather than starting from a blank canvas.
